## Findings that change the next action

1. **The distributed inertias should remain centroidal.** Integrating Tables 3.5–3.6 and adding the parallel-axis terms between section centres gives whole-wing centroidal inertias of 5.25019328e-5 and 6.41286323e-5 kg m². Table 2.21 gives 5.251e-5 and 6.411e-5: agreement within 0.016% and 0.030%. Treating the distributed values as inertias about the literal elastic axis, Cea=0 and Nea=0.001 m, instead gives **4.26614160e-5 and 6.21692251e-5 kg m²**. The previous report's 4.2180e-5 and 6.1708e-5 used the chordline normal offset and were not the literal EA calculation. Both alternatives remain recorded in JSON. Weighted chordwise centres are 0.0343342 and 0.0407015 m from the leading edge, matching Tables 2.9 and 2.21. This supports retaining the centroidal interpretation; it does not prove the original simulation setup. Exact integration also gives **0.0724994064 kg for the 75% wing versus 0.0726 kg printed in Table 2.21 (-0.13856%)**. Preserve this source discrepancy without mass rescaling. See printed pages 45, 52, 64–65; `thesis.txt` lines 2943–2950, 3323–3346, 4004–4043.
2. **The normal CG entries support their literal current interpretation.** Weighted Ncg is 0.00379201/0.00368203 m, matching Table 2.9's CG distances from the chordline. Thus the prose assertion that both CG coordinates are referenced to the elastic axis is imprecise in the normal direction; it does not justify adding Nea to the table entries. The separately recorded 0.001/0.005 m Nea discrepancy remains.
3. **The section camber sign is supported.** Representation 2 in Figure 3.3 has alpha_A = -alpha_L0. Section `alpha=+4°` therefore represents a zero-lift incidence of -4°. For span +z, chord +x and normal -y, positive incidence is body beta; body alpha tilts the flow along the span. Figure 2.13 confirms that the experimental incidence is a turntable rotation around z. This is the reconstruction's coordinate mapping. The original computational body beta and `.pnt` input remain undocumented; the experimental incidence does not prove those settings. No source evidence supports reversing either sign to fit flutter. See printed pages 29, 61 and 67; PDF pages 42, 74 and 80.
4. **The main destruction-test wings had no accelerometers.** Section 2.2.13 explicitly says they were uninstrumented and that GVT accelerometers were removed before active wind-tunnel tests. This supports excluding concentrated instrumentation masses for this experimental comparison. It does not establish which exact input generated the published computational boundaries. See printed pages 29–30 / PDF 42–43, `thesis.txt` lines 2153–2188.
5. **The source's static-unbalance explanation contradicts its tables.** Tables 2.20–2.21 give CG-minus-EA chordwise offsets of 10.64 mm for the 25% wing and 4.39 mm for the 75% wing, although the root-locus discussion calls the latter's static unbalance larger. Its elastic axis is farther aft of the aerodynamic centre, which is a distinct effect. Preserve the numeric tables; do not adjust them to satisfy the prose. See printed pages 52, 75–76; `thesis.txt` lines 4522–4539 and 4590–4598.
6. **The 25% discrepancy changes the shape of the stability boundary.** Section 4.2 and Figure 4.10 explicitly identify cases 1–4 at physical incidence 4.17°; the legend identifies Case 4 as solid. The qualitative Case 4 comparison is therefore supported, while the original body beta remains unknown. Figure 4.10 shows the first torsion branch beginning near 35.1 Hz, crossing near 21 Hz and staying unstable over the displayed continuation. The current coarse-grid branch instead has an unstable pocket from about 23.55 to 27.61 m/s at roughly 26.9–25.1 Hz. The 75% plotted crossing is roughly 19.5 Hz. These are visual frequency estimates, not digitized reference values or validation tolerances. Keep them separate from the precisely tabulated Case 4 speeds of 33.21 and 24.58 m/s. The similar zero-speed starting frequencies support investigating finite-speed forces and loaded-state effects. See Figure 4.10, printed page 78 / PDF 91, and section 4.2, printed pages 75–76.
